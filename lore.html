Note: These legends are reconstructed from what the NPCs say and from the mod's descriptions. They are the world's folklore: some parts may be “made up”, as the Bard would say.

Petronite

A blue-gray ore pulses beneath the deepslate. The City Guard was the first to whisper the rumor: “there is a glowing blue ore beneath the deepslate”. The Wizard claims that Petronite “holds an ancient magic deep underground” and that whoever melds it with a diamond forges the metal of legends.

The Dawnblade

The Knight swears to protect the road until the dawn rises again. His sword, the Dawnblade, only shines for those who do not fear the dark: “whoever forges their sword with petronite fears no darkness”.

The Storm

The great runic axe of the City Guard holds the echo of distant thunder: the Stormaxe calls lightning down on its enemies.

The Fairy and her heart

The Fairy is the voice of the forests. She says that “a Fairy Heart is forged from a totem, diamonds, gold and Ender pearls” and that the forests whisper secrets to her when the fireflies glow. The Fairy Heart saves you from death five times.

The Staff

The Wizard studies the runes and petronite. Beware, he says, of “staring into the void for too long”. From this comes the Void Armor, which knows more than it admits.

The Ogre and the staff

The PlayStation Staff Ogre wields a staff of inexplicable origin. Legend says it came from another world.

The Awakening of Dragoso

A ritual of netherrack, petronite and three dragon heads awakens the half-dragon, half-bear beast. As it rises from the ground, the world hears: “DRAGOSO HAS AWAKENED!”. From its wings are born the Dragon Wings, the prize of whoever defeats it.

The Glacial Boss

Beneath the snow, at the center of the Glacial Arena, sleeps a colossal beast made of frost, with a white mane and blue spikes. Around a block of petronite and ice, a Nether Star wakes it: “THE GLACIAL BOSS HAS AWAKENED!”.

The dragons

The four dragons —Fire, Ice, Earth and Wind— sleep as eggs in the Dragon Portal, on the outer islands of the End. It is said that the dragon chooses its owner: whoever places the egg, and whoever raises it to adulthood, is its flying companion.
